## Two observations from the numbers, recorded because they will matter later

**1. `frame-header` runs ~2× the cases of the other two targets, and that is a property of the
generator, not of coverage.** A header case is ~50–600 bytes and its parse is a handful of integer
checks; a GGUF case builds metadata and tensor descriptors, and a wire-body case builds a real
FlatBuffer and bit-flips it. Cases-per-second is therefore not a quality signal, and **CPU-seconds,
not iteration counts, are what the DoD is denominated in** — which is why the driver reports both
and the table totals the former.

**2. Shard 2's `frame-header` leg ran 909 M cases against a ~550 M median (+65 %).** That is runner
variance (CPU model, contention), not a signal about that seed. It is left in the table unsmoothed
because a receipt that quietly normalises its own numbers is worth less than one that shows them —
but it is also the reason no per-seed conclusion should be drawn from throughput.

## What this receipt does and does not establish

**Does:** the three parsers survived **9.36 billion** structure-aware hostile inputs in this leg —
**11.32 billion cumulatively** — without a panic, an abort, or an unbounded allocation, on the exact
tree that shipped the M4·1 fixes (sha `581da25`).

**Does not:**

* **It is not 24 CPU-hours.** It is 2.4 cumulative, and the DoD stays open.
* **It is not coverage-guided fuzzing.** `hydra-fuzz` has no feedback loop telling it which mutations
  reach new code; depth here comes from time on eight fixed seed streams. `cargo-fuzz` over the same
  entry points is an owed additive upgrade (§8).
* **It does not exercise `llama.cpp`'s own GGUF parser**, which is reached when a shard is actually
  loaded. Only Hydra's pure-Rust `hydra-modelsvc` parser is under test.
* **Absence of a crash is not absence of a bug** — it is absence of *this class* (panics, aborts,
  unbounded allocations) under *these generators*.

## Reproducing any case

```
cargo run --release -p hydra-fuzz --bin hydra-fuzz -- --target gguf --seed 2000006 --replay 412345
```
