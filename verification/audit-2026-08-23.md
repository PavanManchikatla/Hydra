## The central thesis — ratified as a standing lesson

> **The checklist's rows outrun their assertions.**

M4·1 built `docs/SECURITY-CHECKLIST.md` on the principle that *a documented invariant nothing checks
is not an invariant*. The audit accepted that and pushed it one level further: **a row that claims
more than its test proves is the same failure wearing the test's clothes.** `verify()` was the
example — the checklist row read "manifest signature verified before any shard weights are read",
and a test proved exactly that sentence, while the signature was checked against a key the attacker
supplies.

**Ratified as PROJECT_STATE §7.31:**

> **A test whose name promises more than its assertion is worse than no test, because it terminates
> inquiry.** A missing test leaves a visible hole. An over-promising test fills the hole with an
> answer, and the reader — reasonably — stops looking.

Wave 5 exists to walk every checklist row and either tighten the test to the row's claim or narrow
the row's language to what the test proves.

## Threat-model ruling (governs every severity below)

**v1 keeps the honest-worker assumption — but it is now an explicit, documented, load-bearing
assumption rather than something the identity story implies away.**

Landed in **BLUEPRINT §1.9** and the **spec's scope section**:

* mTLS + signed manifests protect against **outsiders and accidents**, not a **compromised stage**.
* A worker holding a valid cluster certificate can lie about its computation; v1 has no mechanism
  that detects it.
* What rests on the assumption is named: teacher-forced recovery trusts a survivor's replayed
  boundaries; the solver trusts reported capability; `BOUNDARY_COPY` durability trusts the copier;
  S_P is the single source of sampled tokens.
* **Worker-compromise resistance is v2 scope.**

**The criticals were fixed anyway** — none of them depends on worker trust. Each is reachable by an
outsider, a hostile file, or an accident.

## Two findings that pause for ratification

Per rule 11, anything touching protocol semantics stops and proposes an amendment rather than
choosing. Both of these are **genuine silences in the spec that the audit correctly identified** —
not implementation bugs:

* **H1 — attempt-fence bounds.** The spec fences activation attempts by `activation_attempt_id` but
  never bounds it. A proposed amendment accompanies Wave 1's report.
* **M13 — reset/attempt-floor gap.** `RESET_RECOVERY_ATTEMPT` increments `recovery_id` but the spec
  does not say what happens to the attempt floor across a reset.

Neither is fixed unilaterally. The model, then the code, follow the spec — not the reverse.
